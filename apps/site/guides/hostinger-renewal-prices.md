Hostinger advertises a low monthly price, but it is the price for the first term, paid up front. When the term ends, the plan renews at a higher monthly rate. That is normal for shared hosting. It still matters when you compare hosts, because the first bill and the fifth-year bill are very different.

This guide sets out what Hostinger's web hosting plans cost at sign-up and at renewal, then compares them with flat-priced static hosting for a simple site. We read every price on each vendor's own page on 25 November 2026. Hostinger was running a sale that day, so we leave its first-term prices out: they change with each promotion. The renewal prices are the ones to plan around.

## TL;DR

- **Hostinger Premium renews at $10.99 a month,** or $131.88 a year, on 12, 24 and 48-month terms. The 1-month plan is $12.99 a month. The low advertised price covers the first term only, paid up front.
- **Static.app Starter is $60 a year, every year,** for 2 sites with unlimited traffic. That is less than half of Hostinger Premium's renewal price.
- **A long first term can still win early on.** If Hostinger's first-term price for a 48-month plan is under $240, it costs less than Static.app Starter for those four years. From the first renewal, Static.app costs less every year.
- **Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages are free** for static sites, with limits. GitHub Pages is not allowed for business or ecommerce sites.
- **Choose Hostinger** if you need WordPress, PHP, a database or email at the host. **Choose a static host** if your site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript and you want the same bill every year.

## Worked example: one small site, year after year

Hostinger's first term is a discount. Every year after it is billed at the renewal rate. So the fair comparison is the yearly cost once the first term ends, plus the first term at whatever price is on offer when you buy.

| How you pay | Per year, after the first term | 4 years at that rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hostinger Premium, 1-month plan | $155.88 | $623.52 |
| Hostinger Premium, 12 to 48-month terms | $131.88 | $527.52 |
| Static.app Starter, billed yearly | $60 | $240 |
| Cloudflare Pages, free plan | $0 | $0 |

How the Hostinger rows add up: 12 × $12.99 = $155.88 a year on the 1-month plan, and 12 × $10.99 = $131.88 a year at the renewal rate.

**Where the first term fits in:** on a 48-month plan you pay the first-term price once, up front, and the renewal rate starts in year five. If that first-term price is under $240, Hostinger costs less than Static.app Starter for the first four years. On a 12-month term, the renewal rate starts in year two, and Static.app Starter costs $71.88 a year less from then on.

The domain is left out of every row. Hostinger includes one for the first year only, on terms of 12 months or longer. With a static host you buy or bring your own.

## Flat-priced static hosting

A static host serves files: HTML, CSS, JavaScript and images. There is no PHP, no database and no WordPress. If your site is built with a static site generator, exported from a site builder, or written by hand or with a coding agent, that is all it needs.

### 1. Static.app

[Static.app](https://static.app/) hosts static sites at a flat price. You upload a ZIP or a folder, and the site is live with SSL.

![Static.app's pricing page with yearly billing, showing a 7-day free trial and the Starter plan at $5 a month, $60 billed annually.](/guides/hostinger-renewal-prices/static-app.jpg "Static.app's pricing page, billed yearly, 25 November 2026")

**Pricing:** Starter is $60 a year for 2 sites and 500 MB of storage. Medium is $120 a year for 7 sites and 3 GB. Large is $180 a year for 30 sites and 10 GB. Month to month, the same plans are $6, $12 and $18; the page says yearly billing saves $12, $24 and $36. Every paid plan lists unlimited traffic, custom domains, SSL, forms and analytics. There is a 7-day free trial with no card. It also has an API and an MCP server, so a coding agent can publish to it.

**Limitations:** no free plan after the trial. No server code and no database, so no WordPress. It does not build your site from Git: you upload the built folder, or deploy with its API. 500 MB on Starter is small for an image-heavy site. No email hosting.

**Good for:** a site that is already static, where you want the same price every year and no build setup.

### 2. Cloudflare Pages

[Cloudflare Pages](https://pages.cloudflare.com/) hosts static sites on Cloudflare's network. The free plan allows 500 builds a month, one build at a time, according to Cloudflare's limits page.

**Pricing:** free for most small sites. Paid Cloudflare plans raise the build and file limits. [Cloudflare Pages pricing in 2026](/guides/cloudflare-pages-pricing/) has the details.

**Limitations:** you work in Cloudflare's dashboard or its command-line tool. No email hosting and no PHP.

**Good for:** developers who are fine setting up a Git repository or a command-line deploy and want to pay nothing.

### 3. GitHub Pages

[GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com/) publishes a site from a GitHub repository.

![GitHub's documentation page on GitHub Pages limits, saying GitHub Pages is not intended for running an online business or ecommerce site.](/guides/hostinger-renewal-prices/github-pages.jpg "GitHub Pages limits, GitHub Docs, 25 November 2026")

**Pricing:** free with public repositories on GitHub Free. Private repositories need GitHub Pro, Team or Enterprise.

**Limitations:** GitHub's docs say Pages is not intended or allowed for running an online business or ecommerce site. Published sites can be at most 1 GB, with a soft bandwidth limit of 100 GB a month and a soft limit of 10 builds an hour.

**Good for:** personal sites, documentation and open-source project pages.

### 4. Netlify

[Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/) builds and hosts sites from Git, with serverless functions and forms. Its pricing page lists Free at $0, Personal at $9 a month and Pro at $20 a month, with usage measured in credits. Personal says you top up credits as needed, so the bill can change with traffic and builds, unlike a flat plan. [Netlify pricing in 2026](/guides/netlify-pricing/) explains how credits work.

## Which should you choose?

- **You run WordPress, or need PHP, a database or email:** Hostinger, or another shared host. A static host can't run these. Take the longest term you are comfortable paying up front, and note the renewal date and price.
- **Your site is plain files and you want one predictable price:** Static.app. $60 a year for Starter, every year.
- **Your site is plain files and you want to pay nothing:** Cloudflare Pages, or GitHub Pages for a personal or open-source site.
- **You are on Hostinger and your site is static or could be:** compare your renewal bill with a flat static plan before the term ends. [Moving WordPress to a static site](/guides/wordpress-to-static-site/) covers the export.
- **You want builds from Git with serverless functions:** Netlify or [Vercel](/guides/vercel-pricing/), with usage-based bills.

## FAQ

### How much does Hostinger cost after the first term?

On 25 November 2026, Hostinger's page said Premium renews at $10.99 a month, Unlimited at $16.99 and Cloud Startup at $25.99, for 12, 24 and 48-month terms. The 1-month plans were $12.99, $18.99 and $27.99 a month.

### Is Hostinger's advertised monthly price real?

Yes, for the first term, paid up front for the whole term. After that term, Premium renews at $10.99 a month on 12 to 48-month terms.

### Why do hosting renewal prices go up?

Shared hosts discount the first term to win sign-ups, and charge their regular rate from the first renewal. Hostinger shows the renewal price under each plan, so you can see it before you buy.

### Is static hosting cheaper than Hostinger?

After the first term, yes. Hostinger Premium renews at $131.88 a year, against $60 for Static.app Starter. During a long discounted first term, Hostinger can cost less. Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages are free.

### Can I host a WordPress site on a static host?

Not as WordPress. You can export a WordPress site to static files and host those, but forms, comments and search then need other tools. [Moving WordPress to a static site](/guides/wordpress-to-static-site/) walks through it.
