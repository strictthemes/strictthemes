Email warm-up and inbox placement testing are often sold together, but they do different jobs. A warm-up tool sends and engages with email from your mailbox so it builds a sending history. A placement test sends one real message to a list of test inboxes and tells you which folder each copy landed in. One acts on your reputation. The other measures where you stand.

This guide compares six warm-up tools and shows where a placement test fits. We read every price below on each vendor's own pricing page on 20 November 2026. We did not run any of these tools, so we compare what vendors publish, not results.

## TL;DR

- **Warm-up pricing differs by vendor.** MailReach is €25 per mailbox a month as shown, and Folderly's core product is $96 per mailbox a month on a yearly plan. Warmy, Instantly, lemlist and InboxAlly price by plan.
- **Instantly bundles warm-up with sending,** from $47 a month with unlimited accounts and unlimited warm-up. It is a cold email sending platform, so you also move your sending to it.
- **InboxAlly starts at $149 a month.** It trains inbox providers with engagement from its seed inboxes, and is built for senders with a placement problem.
- **A placement test is a different tool.** It does not warm anything up. It tells you whether mail lands in the inbox, in Promotions or in spam. Unspam charges $0.50 per inbox placement test on its Custom plan, with a free plan that includes 3.
- **Which comes first depends on the sender.** Warm-up is sold for new cold-email mailboxes with no history. If your domain already sends to real subscribers, a test is a quicker first check.

## What each kind of tool does

| | Warm-up tool | Inbox placement test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it does | Sends, opens, replies to and rescues mail between your mailbox and a network of other mailboxes | Sends your real message to seed inboxes and reports where each copy landed |
| Aim | Build sending history and reputation | Measure placement now |
| How it is priced | Per mailbox at some vendors, per plan at others | Per test, or a monthly allowance of tests |
| Needs your mailbox connected | Yes | No, you send from your usual tool |

MailReach describes its warm-up in three steps. You connect your mailboxes, it starts conversations with high-reputation inboxes from its network, and your emails are opened, marked as important, taken out of spam and replied to. It then tracks a reputation score per address.

A placement test reports the result, as Unspam puts it: Inbox, Promotions, Spam, or Missing, across Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo and more.

## Email warm-up tools

### 1. MailReach: best for warming a handful of mailboxes

[MailReach](https://www.mailreach.co/) is a warm-up tool with a spam test attached. It says its network has more than 30,000 accounts.

![MailReach's pricing page, with the All-In-One plan at 25 euros per mailbox per month.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/mailreach.jpg "MailReach's pricing page, 20 November 2026")

The All-In-One plan is €25 per mailbox a month, shown in euros, and the Annually switch says it saves 20%. It includes warm-up with up to 100 emails a day, 20 spam test credits, a reputation dashboard and domain and inbox health checks for blacklists, SPF, DKIM and DMARC. A separate Spam Tester option sells test credits from 20 to 10,000 a month. Agencies are asked to ask for custom pricing.

What it does not do: the price scales with every mailbox you warm, so ten mailboxes is ten times the price. The page also shows an Autumn sale of 20% off the first 30 days.

**Good for:** a few sending mailboxes that need warm-up and the occasional test together.

### 2. Warmy: best for a plan with expert support and extra tools

[Warmy](https://www.warmy.io/) sells warm-up as part of a wider deliverability platform, with an AI assistant it calls Adeline. Its pricing page starts plans at $49 a month for B2B senders, B2C senders or a custom plan, and offers a 7-day free trial with no card.

![Warmy's pricing page, with plans starting from $49 a month and a 7-day free trial.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/warmy.jpg "Warmy's pricing page, 20 November 2026")

Every plan lists placement test credits, a reputation tracker, Google Postmaster data, email verification and DMARC monitoring. Warmy says it has a network of more than 1 million mailboxes and more than 500,000 US seed mailboxes. Pricing is by the volume of warm-up emails you need. Its Black Friday banner announces 50% off annual plans.

What it does not do: the page does not say how many placement tests a plan includes. It tells you to confirm the monthly allowance with Warmy's team. The $49 starting price is for "plans starting from", so check your own volume on the trial.

**Good for:** senders who want one subscription for warm-up, testing and monitoring, with a named expert.

### 3. Instantly: best if you also want to send cold email from the same tool

[Instantly](https://instantly.ai/) is a cold email platform with warm-up built in. Growth is $47 a month, Hypergrowth is $107 and Light Speed is $358, shown with monthly billing.

![Instantly's pricing page, with Growth at $47, Hypergrowth at $107 and Light Speed at $358 a month.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/instantly.jpg "Instantly's pricing page, 20 November 2026")

Growth lists unlimited email accounts and unlimited email warm-up, with 1,000 uploaded contacts and 5,000 emails a month. Its inbox placement tool gives 2 free tests, then unlimited tests on a paid plan, across Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo.

What it does not do: it is a sending platform, so warm-up comes with moving your outreach to Instantly, and the contact and email limits on each plan apply. It is not a standalone warm-up service for mail you send from another tool.

**Good for:** teams running cold outreach with many mailboxes, where per-mailbox warm-up pricing would add up.

### 4. lemlist (lemwarm): best if you already use lemlist

[lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/) bundles its warm-up and deliverability hub into its outreach plans. The Email plan starts at 55€ a month billed yearly, shown for 50,000 emails a month, or 69€ billed monthly. Multichannel is 87€ yearly or 109€ monthly.

![lemlist's pricing page, with the Email plan at 55 euros and Multichannel at 87 euros a month on yearly billing.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/lemlist.jpg "lemlist's pricing page, shown in euros, 20 November 2026")

The Email plan lists email warm-up and a deliverability hub, with deliverability testing and alerts. Prices are shown in euros for our location, and the page says local currency billing is available.

What it does not do: you pay for the whole outreach platform, so it is more than you need if you only want warm-up.

**Good for:** teams that already use lemlist for outbound.

### 5. InboxAlly: best for a placement problem you need repaired

[InboxAlly](https://www.inboxally.com/) works differently. It says it teaches inbox providers such as Gmail that your messages are valued, by engaging with them from seed inboxes. It markets repair of messages stuck in spam or Promotions.

![InboxAlly's pricing page, with Starter at $149, Plus at $645 and Premium at $1,190 a month.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/inboxally.jpg "InboxAlly's pricing page, 20 November 2026")

Starter is $149 a month for 100 seed emails a day and 1 sender profile. Plus is $645 for 500 a day and 5 profiles. Premium is $1,190 for 1,000 a day and 10 profiles, with phone support. Enterprise is custom. It offers a 10-day trial with no card.

What it does not do: it costs far more than the others, and tiers are sold by seed volume and profiles. It is meant for higher-volume senders.

**Good for:** a sender with a real inbox-placement problem and a budget to fix it.

### 6. Folderly: best for done-for-you fixes

[Folderly](https://folderly.com/) is a suite. The core Folderly product is $96 per mailbox a month on a yearly plan, and it is sold through a demo. Its pages describe technical and content checks, placement monitoring and guidance.

![Folderly's products page, with Folderly at $96 per mailbox a month and Inbox Insights with a free plan.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/folderly.jpg "Folderly's products page, 20 November 2026")

Inbox Insights is a separate placement test product. It has a free plan with 2 tests a month, $79 a month for 100 tests, or $799 yearly. Pulse, which alerts you about placement changes, is free.

What it does not do: pricing is spread across several products, so it is harder to compare, and the core product is per mailbox.

**Good for:** B2B teams that want a vendor to fix problems, not just report them.

## Where a placement test fits

### Unspam: a test, not a warm-up tool

[Unspam](https://unspam.email/) does not warm up mailboxes. It measures. You send your real campaign to its seed list across Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo and more, and it reports where each copy landed. It also scores the message against spam signals and renders it in more than 60 email clients.

![Unspam's pricing page, with the free plan at $0 a month and 21 tests included.](/guides/email-warm-up-tools/unspam.jpg "Unspam's pricing page, 20 November 2026")

The Free plan is $0 and includes 21 tests a month, with 3 inbox placements. The Custom plan is a monthly plan you size by test type: the page priced a default mix of 25 tests at $6.40 a month, with $0.50 for an inbox placement and $0.18 for a spam test. It never charges per inbox or per seat. DMARC monitoring is free for the first domain, then $6 for each extra domain. The pricing page runs a Black Friday offer of 35% off new subscriptions until 1 December; see [Black Friday 2026 deals](/guides/black-friday-2026-deals/).

What it does not do: it does not warm up mailboxes, send conversations or repair a reputation. It tells you where you land, so you can decide whether a warm-up tool is needed. For how it works with a sender, see [Resend pricing in 2026](/guides/resend-pricing/).

**Good for:** newsletter and product senders who want to check placement before and after a change, without a per-mailbox bill.

## Which should you choose?

- **You have new mailboxes for cold outreach and no sending history:** a warm-up tool. MailReach for a few mailboxes, Instantly or lemlist if you also want the sending platform.
- **You want warm-up, tests and monitoring from one vendor:** Warmy, checking the test allowance on its trial.
- **Your mail already lands in spam or Promotions and you can pay to fix it:** InboxAlly or Folderly.
- **You send newsletters or product email from a domain with real subscribers:** start with a placement test, since there is no new mailbox to warm. Then decide.
- **You use a warm-up tool that has no placement tests:** add a test now and then to check where a real message lands. Several of the tools above include tests.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between email warm-up and inbox placement testing?

Warm-up builds a sending history by exchanging engaged emails between your mailbox and a network. A placement test sends one real message to seed inboxes and reports which folder each copy landed in. One acts on reputation, the other measures where you stand.

### How much does an email warm-up tool cost?

MailReach is €25 per mailbox a month, Folderly's core product is $96 per mailbox, Instantly starts at $47 a month, Warmy at $49 a month, lemlist at 55€ a month and InboxAlly at $149 a month. They bill by different units and currencies, so price your own setup. Prices are on each vendor's page and change.

### Do I need warm-up for a newsletter?

Warm-up tools are sold mainly to cold email senders with new mailboxes. If your domain already sends to real subscribers, a placement test is a quicker way to find out whether your mail reaches the inbox.

### Is a warm-up tool the same as an inbox placement test?

No. Some vendors sell both, such as MailReach, Warmy and Instantly, but they are separate features. Unspam sells only the test.

### Which warm-up tool has a free trial?

Warmy offers a 7-day free trial with no card, and InboxAlly a 10-day trial with no card. Instantly gives 2 free placement tests. Folderly's Inbox Insights has a free plan with 2 tests a month.

### Does Unspam warm up my mailbox?

No. Unspam measures inbox placement, spam signals and email rendering. It does not send or engage with mail to build reputation.
